# Two Types of Problem Properties, Two Different Fixes

Of our 15 properties, 13 had viewings in 2023, with an overall no-show rate of 6.2%. The remaining issues fall into two groups that need different approaches.

## Group 1: High No-Show Rates (Properties 2 and 3)

Property 2 had 3 no-shows out of 16 viewings (18.8%), and property 3 had 2 out of 13 (15.4%), nearly three times the average.

Renters are booking, so demand isn't the issue. The problem is getting them to show up.

We ruled out two possible causes. Amenities don't explain it since other properties with the same features had no no-shows. Price doesn't fully explain it either. Both properties rent for around $3,200, and while expensive properties have a higher no-show rate overall (9.7% vs. 4.1%), property 5 rents for the same amount and had zero no-shows.

This suggests something may be different in how viewings are handled at properties 2 and 3.

**Recommended action:** Compare the viewing process for properties 2 and 3 with property 5. Check who manages the appointments, how bookings are confirmed, and whether renters receive reminders. Add a confirmation reminder the day before and measure the no-show rate again after three months.

Property 3 is currently unavailable, so we should first confirm whether it will return to the market. Property 4 had 1 no-show out of 6 viewings, which is too small a sample to act on. I'd keep monitoring it until we have at least 10 viewings.

## Group 2: Zero Bookings (Properties 14 and 15)

Properties 14 (Saigon, $651/month) and 15 (Hue, $1,343/month) were both available but had zero viewings booked in 2023.

These properties were missing from earlier reports because those queries only included properties with viewings.

This is a different problem. There are no bookings to begin with, so improving reminders won't help. Either renters aren't finding the listings or they're seeing them and choosing not to book.

Both properties also have no amenities recorded. That could be contributing to the issue, but the data doesn't tell us why renters aren't booking.

**Recommended action:** Review both listings from a renter's perspective. Confirm they appear in search, check the photos, and fill in the missing amenities. Also contact the owners to verify the units are still available and accepting viewings.

If either property still has zero bookings 30 days after those fixes, mark it unavailable.

## Why the Distinction Matters

Properties 2 and 3 have bookings but lose renters before the viewing. Properties 14 and 15 aren't getting bookings at all.

For the first group, we need to improve the confirmation process. For the second, we need to investigate listing visibility and quality. Applying the same fix to both would miss the actual problem.

## Reporting Improvement

One thing I’d change going forward is to build every property report from the complete list of properties, rather than from the list of viewings.

That way, properties with zero bookings show up with a count of 0 instead of disappearing from the report. Otherwise, we risk overlooking properties that may need the most attention.
